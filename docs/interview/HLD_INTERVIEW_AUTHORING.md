# Authoring guide: HLD (System Design) interview Q&A pages

Pages live in `C:/Users/kmangali/HLD/interview/topics/hld-<slug>.html`. The reader is a senior engineer (5 to 10 years) preparing for system design interviews. These pages are the QUESTION BANK for the System Design Handbook: the Handbook teaches the topics, these pages ask the questions an interviewer actually asks, with good answers.

## What the user asked for (explicit)
- Cover ALL the HLD interview questions on every topic in your page's scope. No cap on the count. Go broad and complete.
- NO fixed answer format. Each answer may be a definition, an example, a short explanation, a detailed explanation, a comparison table, a small diagram, a numbered flow, whatever fits that question best. Vary it. Do not force "direct answer / deeper / remember" blocks.
- Do NOT include case-study design problems (URL shortener, rate limiter, chat, news feed, etc). They already exist in the HLD Handbook. Questions about the building blocks of those (for example "how does a token bucket work") are fine; "Design Twitter" is not.
- Focus on interview questions of the kinds: what is X, why X (with a concrete example), how does X work, compare X and Y, when would you pick X, what goes wrong with X, situational questions ("your service sees ... what do you do"), real-life scenarios, and practical problems that really happened in industry (well-known outages and post-mortems, described accurately and hedged where unsure).
- Plain, natural, easy-to-follow English. Define jargon where first used. Concrete numbers and examples beat abstractions.

## Hard requirements (only these)
- One complete HTML page with the skeleton below, at least 25 questions and 2,500 words (aim far higher: complete coverage of the scope is the goal; typical pages will have 40 to 100 questions).
- Each question is a collapsible: `<details class="iq" id="unique-id"><summary>Question text? <span class="lvl b">Basic</span></summary><div class="ans"> ...free-form answer... </div></details>`. The badge is optional, use `lvl b` Basic, `lvl i` Intermediate, `lvl s` Senior if you want. Situational/real-life ones may use `class="iq scn"`. The Q-number is generated by CSS; never type it.
- Group questions under `<h2 id="...">` sections (every h2 needs an id). Start with a short "How to use this page" paragraph and finish with an optional compact "Quick revision table" (one-line answers) if useful.
- Facts must be accurate. Where version- or vendor-specific, say so or hedge ("typically", "about"). For industry incidents, state only what you are confident about (company, year, headline cause) and hedge details. Do not invent numbers; list anything you are unsure of in your final report.
- Useful extras you may use freely inside answers: `<table>`, `<ul>/<ol>`, `<pre><code>`, `<div class="say"><b>Say:</b> ...</div>`, `<div class="trap"><b>Trap:</b> ...</div>`, `<div class="tl">...</div>`, and inline SVG diagrams `<figure class="diagram"><svg viewBox="0 0 W H" role="img" aria-label="..."> ... </svg><figcaption>...</figcaption></figure>` (classes `box cache db ext hit miss`, lines `arrow arrow-g arrow-r dash`, text `t-sm t-b t-c`; marker defs ah/ahg/ahr in the hidden svg). Diagrams are encouraged where they teach, not required. Keep labels from overlapping (about 8 px per character).
- Do not use `class="why"`, `class="fu"` boxes. No external URLs, fonts or images. The only script allowed is `<script src="../../theme.js"></script>` in the head. Never write the names of unsafe DOM APIs in text (the checker greps). No em dashes needed; plain punctuation.
